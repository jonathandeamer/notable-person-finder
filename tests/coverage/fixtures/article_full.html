Ada Lovelace and the Analytical Engine

A profile of Ada Lovelace and her notes on Babbage's Analytical Engine.

By Jane Writer

Ada Lovelace is often described as the first computer programmer. Her extensive notes on Charles Babbage's Analytical Engine include what is widely recognized as the first published algorithm intended for a machine.

Beyond the algorithm itself, Lovelace imagined general-purpose computing: machines that could manipulate symbols, not only numbers. That insight anticipates modern software far more clearly than many of her contemporaries.

Curators of a recent exhibition emphasize how her correspondence reveals a rigorous mathematical practice and a public intellectual role in Victorian science, rather than a purely symbolic figure in computing history.
